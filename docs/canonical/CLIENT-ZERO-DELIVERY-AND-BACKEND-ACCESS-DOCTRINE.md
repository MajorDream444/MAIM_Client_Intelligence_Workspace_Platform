# MAIM / Stop the Bleed — Client Zero Delivery and Backend Access Doctrine

Status: CANONICAL PLATFORM DOCTRINE  
Owner: Major Dream Williams  
Effective: 2026-09-24  
Applies to: intake agents, officers, builders, operators, reviewers, project agents, client workspaces, and governed client engagements

## Purpose

This doctrine converts the lessons of the Sri Shakti Shala engagement into reusable operating rules for MAIM Client Intelligence Workspaces and Stop the Bleed delivery.

Sheetal is Client Zero. Her engagement is both a client delivery and a bounded learning environment used to discover:

- what belongs in the standard system;
- what must remain configurable;
- what becomes a template;
- what becomes deterministic automation;
- what warrants an agent;
- what requires human discernment;
- what belongs in maintenance or a later phase;
- what constitutes a chargeable change request;
- and what conditions must be met before ownership and backend access transfer.

Client Zero flexibility is research input. It is not the permanent commercial delivery model.

## Governing principles

### 1. Systems before aesthetics

MAIM builds operating systems that must first perform their agreed function.

Design should be coherent, attractive, accessible, and aligned with the client. Visual preference does not override system integrity, delivery controls, source truth, testing, security, or handoff readiness.

A client may redesign the system after ownership transfers or commission a separate approved design phase. An active build is not an unrestricted design sandbox.

### 2. Transparency does not require edit access

A client must be able to see:

- agreed scope;
- progress;
- deliverables;
- decisions;
- dependencies;
- blockers;
- requests;
- payments;
- ownership;
- acceptance state;
- and handoff readiness.

That visibility is provided through the Client Intelligence Workspace, reports, previews, review environments, approval records, and client-safe source links.

Visibility does not imply permission to modify implementation, production configuration, canonical records, integrations, workflows, or source code during active delivery.

### 3. Build control remains with the operator until handoff

During BUILD and HANDOFF modes, the designated MAIM/operator team controls:

- implementation repositories;
- production deployment;
- integration configuration;
- canonical source mappings;
- schemas and migrations;
- automation logic;
- security and access policies;
- release evidence;
- and change sequencing.

This protects continuity, provenance, rollback ability, testing, privacy, security, and commercial scope.

The client provides truth, approvals, assets, decisions, credentials in approved channels, and feedback. The client does not directly alter active implementation unless a written engagement rule expressly grants a bounded contributor role.

### 4. Ownership transfers at acceptance

The lifecycle is:

```text
BUILD
→ HANDOFF
→ OWNER
```

At formal handoff, subject to the engagement terms, the client receives the agreed code, documentation, account map, data, keys, repositories or repository copy, operating procedures, known limitations, and training.

After transfer, the client may operate, alter, reconnect, redesign, or hire another team to maintain the system. MAIM is not responsible for ungoverned post-handoff changes unless a maintenance agreement says otherwise.

Before handoff, direct backend modification is not client ownership; it is an uncontrolled change to an active delivery environment.

## Backend access policy

| Mode | Client access | Client authority | MAIM/operator responsibility |
|---|---|---|---|
| BUILD | Workspace, approved documents, previews, review forms, request intake | Review, supply truth, approve, reject, request | Build, test, sequence, secure, document |
| HANDOFF | Everything above plus guided administrative review and ownership checklist | Accept deliverables, establish client-owned accounts, confirm operators | Transfer, train, replace operator-owned dependencies, verify |
| OWNER | Agreed repositories/code, accounts, data, documentation and operational access | Operate and change the inherited system | Only obligations in an active support agreement |

Default rule: deny direct write access to production and implementation systems during BUILD. Grant the least privilege required for an explicitly assigned role.

## Change governance

Every request must be classified before work begins:

- **Bug / Correction** — agreed behavior is broken or delivered inaccurately.
- **Founder Review** — client authority or interpretation is required.
- **Revision** — an included adjustment within the contracted revision allowance.
- **Change Request** — valuable work outside agreed completion requirements.
- **Future / Phase 2** — later capability that does not block the current handoff.
- **Owner Maintenance** — post-handoff update to a functioning delivered system.

Decision test:

```text
Is this necessary to correct or complete agreed work?

YES → current build, subject to the agreed revision and acceptance rules
NO  → change request, later phase, maintenance, or client-owned post-handoff work
```

Speed does not eliminate value. A request remains chargeable when tools make it faster if it adds scope, responsibility, risk, judgment, testing, integration, or operational burden.

## Mechanism selection

Use the least complex safe mechanism.

| Work type | Default mechanism |
|---|---|
| Stable known rule | Hard-coded function |
| Repeatable trigger and action | Deterministic automation |
| Ambiguous classification, retrieval, or synthesis | Bounded agent |
| Doctrine, sacredness, publication, financial authority, or scope approval | Human decision |
| Missing identity or intent | Assigned client/team action |
| New business direction | Change request or later phase |

Not everything needs an agent. Intelligence is used where interpretation materially improves the system. Deterministic work remains deterministic.

## Intake standard

Before implementation, intake must establish:

- desired business outcome;
- audience and priority client;
- offers and revenue path;
- journey the audience must take;
- systems and accounts already in use;
- source material and its owner;
- who identifies, approves, and maintains media;
- rights, privacy, sacredness, and publication constraints;
- decision authority;
- client responsibilities and deadlines;
- included deliverables;
- explicit exclusions;
- revision allowance;
- change-request rule;
- ongoing maintenance needs;
- acceptance criteria;
- ownership-transfer requirements;
- and definition of handoff.

The intake record becomes the foundation for configuration, scope, implementation, progress measurement, the client workspace, acceptance, and handoff.

## Client Zero extraction rule

Extract methods, not client doctrine.

```text
Client-specific identity, medicine, content, and truth
→ remain with that client

Reusable operating pattern
→ MAIM / Stop the Bleed asset

Repeatable agent behavior
→ governed skill, tool, or workflow

Deterministic verification
→ test, script, or automation
```

A Client Zero exception is reviewed after delivery and classified as:

- remove;
- retain for that client only;
- make configurable;
- promote to reusable module;
- automate deterministically;
- make agent-assisted;
- make a premium add-on;
- or move to maintenance / later phase.

## Commercial structure

The default commercial separation is:

1. **Stabilize** — diagnose the business, offers, audience, systems, immediate leaks, and decision constraints.
2. **Build and hand over** — deliver the bounded operating system, verify it, document it, transfer it, and obtain acceptance.
3. **Grow the intelligence** — ongoing Vault enrichment, transcription, automation, optimization, content derivatives, analytics, personalization, and new pathways.
4. **High-touch advisory** — continuous founder-level strategy, priority access, bespoke judgment, and active operating involvement.

A fixed first build is not indefinite consulting, unlimited revisions, ongoing design experimentation, perpetual content reconciliation, or an implied intelligent knowledge platform.

Exact prices remain governed by the approved offer, proposal, or contract. This doctrine defines separation of value, not a universal price list.

## Client Workspace obligation

Every governed client workspace should answer within approximately 30 seconds:

- Am I moving forward?
- What has been delivered?
- What is being worked on?
- What is blocked?
- What do I or my team need to do?
- What decisions are waiting for me?
- What changed?
- What is included versus additional?
- What do I own?
- What remains before handoff?

The workspace is the client-facing synthesis layer. It may link to spreadsheets, PDFs, charts, media, mockups, approvals, reports, and source systems without making the client a backend administrator.

## Agent and officer behavior

Agents and officers must:

- preserve this access boundary professionally;
- avoid hostile or personal explanations;
- explain that controlled access protects both parties;
- route client feedback through the approved request and review process;
- distinguish visibility from edit authority;
- identify whether a request is correction, revision, change request, later phase, or owner maintenance;
- never promise backend access or scope outside approved terms;
- never use Client Zero flexibility as precedent for unlimited future delivery;
- and keep the canonical record synchronized with approved decisions.

Recommended client-facing explanation:

> During the active build, your workspace gives you visibility into progress, deliverables, decisions, and requests while the implementation environment remains controlled so we can preserve quality, security, version history, and accountability. At formal handoff, you receive the agreed ownership package and operational access. After that, the system is yours to operate directly or with any team you choose.

## Definition of completion

A first build is complete when the client can use the agreed system to perform its core business purpose; the client and team understand their actions; the implementation is verified; ownership and limitations are documented; the handoff package is accepted; and MAIM is no longer an undocumented operational dependency.

Future potential does not prevent present completion.
