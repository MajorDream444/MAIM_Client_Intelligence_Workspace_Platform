# Founder Contribution Lab

1. Establish whether the session is a synthetic classroom exercise or a real venture. Use fictional identities for education, particularly with minors. Capture goals, roles, existing agreements and disputed assumptions.
2. Read references/contribution-method.md. Attribute Slicing Pie to Mike Moyer; distinguish its general principle from any locally chosen policy. Do not claim certification or reproduce the supplied handbook.
3. Agree eligible contribution categories, market-rate evidence, currency/date, cash versus non-cash treatment, reimbursement handling, review cadence, acknowledgment, dispute process and freeze/exit policy before calculating.
4. Record contributor, date, category, quantity, rate source, fair-market value, amount already paid, unpaid amount, policy version, multiplier if agreed, artifact reference, acknowledgment and dispute state. Prevent double counting expenses as both cash and time.
5. Calculate illustrative units only for acknowledged eligible entries: max(fair-market value minus payment, 0) times an explicitly agreed factor. Exclude disputed entries from the agreed scenario; show their effect separately. If factors or values are missing, leave allocation pending.
6. Divide each participant’s eligible units by total eligible units. If the total is zero, return undefined; do not assign equal percentages. Record denominator, rounding and assumptions. Keep issued shares, invoices and contractual fees in separate records.
7. Walk through a later contribution, reimbursement, dispute, departure and freeze. Do not invent default recovery rules. Require explicit agreement and appropriate professional implementation for real equity or compensation decisions.
8. Output a ledger, assumptions register, provisional scenario, unresolved decisions and reflection: What did we agree, what evidence changed, and what will we do next?

Never let AI decide entitlement, value people by activity volume, issue shares, alter ownership or promise a legally valid equity split.
