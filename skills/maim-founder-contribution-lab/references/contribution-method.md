# Contribution method and source boundaries

Source reviewed: Mike Moyer, Slicing Pie Handbook, uploaded PDF labeled FREE SAMPLE. Printed pages 12–15 explain unpaid contributions, cash/non-cash adjustments and illustrative slices; printed page 33 discusses bootstrapped use and freezing when contributions are paid. This is a sample, not evidence that every detailed valuation or departure rule has been reviewed.

Paraphrase: the method relates provisional allocation to contributions placed at risk. Its tracking units are not issued shares. Record actual eligible unpaid contributions rather than promised future activity. Cash and non-cash may receive different agreed treatment. Do not infer specific multipliers from this sample or silently supply them.

AMA’s proposed teaching ledger borrows that general idea; it is not a certified Slicing Pie implementation. Any valuation, weighting, reimbursement correction, departure or freeze policy must be named, versioned and agreed separately. No licensing, integration or partnership is asserted. Do not distribute the handbook in a public repository.

Synthetic example, using explicitly hypothetical factor 1 for both contributors: A contributes 10 hours at 20 units/hour, paid 50 → 150 eligible units. B contributes unreimbursed cash 100 → 100 eligible units. Total 250 → illustrative 60% / 40%. If A receives a further 50 reimbursement, recompute under the agreed policy: 100 / 200 = 50%, 100 / 200 = 50%. These numbers demonstrate arithmetic, not a recommendation about ownership or cash/non-cash factors.

If an introduction is asserted but value and eligibility are unagreed, record it and its evidence; leave units pending. Distinguish a founder risking unpaid resources from an intermediary seeking a contractual transaction fee.
